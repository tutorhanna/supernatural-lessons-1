🎯 Zero Lesson — Individual Placement & Needs Analysis

Teacher's Guide & Script · Duration: 15–30 minutes

📋 Lesson Overview

Lesson Type Zero Lesson / Placement / Needs Analysis (Individual)
Duration 15–30 minutes (maximum)
Student Level B1 (strong or weak) — to be confirmed during lesson
Format One-on-one (teacher and student)
Main Goals
  • Get to know the student personally
  • Assess English level (speaking, grammar, vocabulary)
  • Understand student's goals, needs, and preferences
  • Explain course format and methodology
  • Decide: group class or individual lessons
Materials Needed This Teacher's Guide, notebook for notes, timer, placement test (optional)
Outcome Clear recommendation for group or individual placement

1. Before the Lesson

✅ Preparation Checklist

  • Review any information you already have about the student (name, age, contact details).
  • Set a timer to manage time (15–30 minutes maximum).
  • Have the American English File PDF available to show if the student asks about the textbook.

⚡ Быстрый опрос (если мало времени)

Всё — устно. Уровень проверяем отдельно (см. Level Check внизу).

1. О себе и целях
  • Расскажи в двух словах о себе: чем занимаешься, чем живёшь?
  • Зачем тебе английский? Что должно измениться, когда выучишь?
  • Есть дедлайн или конкретная ситуация? (собеседование, переезд, экзамен, поездка)
2. Опыт и боли
  • Как учил английский раньше? Что бесило, что заходило?
  • Что для тебя самое трудное в английском?
  • Что помогало больше всего? (сериалы, музыка, зубрёжка, разговор)
3. Группа и комфорт
  • Как относишься к занятиям в группе? Комфортно или лучше один на один?
  • Важно ли, кто в группе — мужчины, женщины, смешанная?
  • Нормально ли ошибаться при других, или это стресс?
  • Исправлять сразу или потом, после того как договоришь?
4. Формат и домашка
  • Готов делать домашку между занятиями или хочешь всё в классе?
  • Что заходит: видео, сериалы, тексты, грамматика, игры?
5. Расписание
  • Сколько раз в неделю готов заниматься и в какие дни/время?
  • Когда хочешь начать?
6. Вопросы ученика
  • Что хочешь узнать обо мне, курсе, оплате, формате — спрашивай.

🎯 Level Check (Speaking) — English

Start with 3 quick multiple-choice questions (to help the student answer). Then move to open questions — with hints in brackets, so the student isn't stuck.

Part 1 — Quick Questions (choose one option or say your own)

1. What do you usually do in the evening?

  • • a) I watch TV / series.   b) I read books.   c) I go for a walk.   d) Something else: ______

2. What did you do last weekend?

  • • a) I stayed at home.   b) I met friends.   c) I went shopping.   d) Something else: ______

3. What are your plans for next month?

  • • a) I'm going to study English.   b) I'm going to travel.   c) I'm going to relax at home.   d) Something else: ______
Part 2 — Let's Talk (use the hints if you get stuck) 📊 Placement Test (10 вопросов) — устно, онлайн

Задайте вопрос. Ученик отвечает устно. Выберите вариант, который наиболее близок к ответу ученика, и сверьте с ключом.

Вопрос (учитель) Вариант A (слабый) Вариант B (средний) Вариант C (сильный)
1. What do you do in your free time? I like... (пауза, односложно) I like reading and going to the cinema. I'm really into photography; I often go hiking on weekends.
2. Tell me about your last holiday. I go to... (путает времена) I went to Turkey. It was good. I swam. I went to Italy last summer. It was amazing — I visited Rome and Florence.
3. What are your plans for next weekend? I will... (базово) I'm going to visit my parents. I'm meeting friends on Saturday, and on Sunday I'm going to relax.
4. Have you ever been to an English-speaking country? No / Yes (без деталей) Yes, I have been to London. It was great. Yes, I've been to the USA twice — I loved New York, but LA was too busy.
5. What do you think about learning English online? Good. / I like. I think it's good because it's convenient. In my opinion, it's effective, but you need self-discipline.
6. If you had more free time, what would you do? I... (не может построить) I would travel more. I'd probably learn to play the guitar and travel more.
7. Can you describe your best friend? He is... (базовые прилагательные) He is tall and funny. He works in IT. He's very reliable and outgoing. We've known each other since school.
8. What did you do yesterday evening? I watch TV. (ошибка времени) I watched a film and cooked dinner. I was watching a series, then I cooked dinner and went for a walk.
9. What's the most difficult part of English for you? Grammar. / Speaking. I think grammar is difficult, especially tenses. I find it hard to speak fluently, and I often forget phrasal verbs.
10. Why do you want to learn English? For work. / For travel. I need English for my job and for traveling. I need it for my career, but I also want to watch films in the original.

🔑 Ключ для учителя

  • Вариант A (слабый) — A2 / слабый B1: Много пауз, базовые структуры, ошибки в временах, ограниченный словарь.
  • Вариант B (средний) — B1: Говорит связно, есть ошибки, но понимает вопрос и отвечает по теме.
  • Вариант C (сильный) — B2 / сильный B1: Беглая речь, сложные структуры, богатый словарь, естественные фразы.

Итог: Если ученик в основном выбирает A → группа слабее. Если B → стандартная B1 группа. Если C → сильная группа или индивидуально.

2. Welcome & First Contact

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Create a warm, welcoming atmosphere
  • Introduce yourself professionally
  • Explain the purpose of the zero lesson
  • Reduce student anxiety

📝 Teacher Script

Greeting (choose one or adapt):

"Hello! It's great to meet you! My name is [Your Name], and I'll be your English teacher."

OR "Hi there! Welcome! I'm [Your Name]. It's wonderful to meet you today."

OR "Good [morning/afternoon/evening]! Thank you for joining me. I'm [Your Name], and I'm excited to get to know you."

Explain the Zero Lesson:

"Today we're having what we call a 'zero lesson' or 'placement lesson'. This is not a regular class — it's a chance for us to get to know each other, for me to understand your English level, and for you to learn about how our course works."

"This lesson will take about 15 to 30 minutes. We'll talk about your goals, your experience with English, and what you want to achieve. I'll also explain our course format, and then we'll decide together whether group classes or individual lessons would be better for you."

Set Expectations & Reduce Anxiety:

"Please don't worry about making mistakes today. This is not a test — it's a conversation. I want to hear your natural English, so I can understand how to help you best."

"Feel free to ask me questions at any time. This is your lesson, and I'm here to support you."

Transition to Next Section:

"Shall we start by getting to know each other a bit? I'd love to hear about you — your background, your work or studies, and what brings you to English learning."

3. Student Background & Goals

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Understand the student's personal and professional background
  • Identify why they want to learn English
  • Discover their specific goals and motivations

📝 Teacher Script

Introduction to Questions:

"Now, I'd like to ask you some questions to understand your situation better. There are no right or wrong answers — I just want to get to know you."

Core Questions (ask all of these):

  • "Can you tell me a little about yourself? For example, where are you from, what do you do, what are your interests?"
    (Listen actively. Take notes on key points: job, studies, hobbies, family if mentioned.)
  • "Why do you want to learn English? What's your main motivation?"
    (Possible answers: work, travel, studies, personal interest, relocation, exams, etc.)
  • "What do you want to be able to do in English after taking this course? What does success look like for you?"
    (Listen for specific goals: "speak fluently", "write emails", "watch movies without subtitles", "pass an exam", etc.)
  • "Do you have a specific deadline or target? For example, do you need English for a job interview, a trip, or an exam by a certain date?"

Additional Questions (choose based on time and conversation flow):

  • "What do you enjoy doing in your free time? Do you have any hobbies or activities you're passionate about?"
  • "Have you ever traveled to an English-speaking country? If yes, what was that experience like?"
  • "Do you use English in your current job or studies? If yes, how? If not, would you like to?"
  • "Are there any specific situations where you really need English? For example, meetings, presentations, emails, travel, socializing?"
  • "What would make you feel that this course was worth your time and money?"

Follow-up & Clarification:

"That's really interesting! Can you tell me more about...?"

"So, if I understand correctly, your main goal is... Is that right?"

"That's a great goal! It's very achievable with regular practice."

Transition to Next Section:

"Thank you for sharing all of that. It really helps me understand what you want to achieve. Now, let's talk a bit about your experience with English learning."

⏱️ Timing: 5–8 minutes
Note-taking: Write down key goals, motivations, and any specific needs mentioned
⚠️ Important: Don't interrupt the student too much. Let them speak naturally. Take notes silently and ask follow-up questions only when they finish a thought.

4. English Learning History

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Understand the student's past experience with English
  • Identify what has worked or not worked before
  • Discover patterns in their learning journey

Core Questions:

  • "When did you start learning English? Was it at school, university, or somewhere else?"
  • "How would you describe your experience with English learning so far? Was it positive, negative, or mixed?"
  • "Have you taken any English courses before? If yes, what were they like? What did you like or dislike about them?"
  • "What has been the most difficult part of learning English for you? Grammar, speaking, listening, vocabulary, pronunciation, something else?"
  • "What has worked well for you in the past? What methods or activities helped you learn?"

Additional Questions (if time permits):

  • "Have you ever studied abroad or lived in an English-speaking country?"
  • "Do you currently use English outside of lessons? For example, watching movies, reading, talking to friends, working?"
  • "Have you ever taken an English proficiency exam? For example, TOEFL, IELTS, Cambridge exams?"
  • "What do you think are your biggest strengths in English? What are you already good at?"
  • "If you could change one thing about how you learned English in the past, what would it be?"

Acknowledge & Validate:

"I understand. Many students feel the same way about [grammar/speaking/listening]. It's very common, and we can definitely work on that."

"That's great that you [mention something positive they said]. We can build on that strength."

"It sounds like you've had some challenging experiences, but you're still motivated to learn. That's really commendable."

Transition to Next Section:

"Thank you for sharing your learning history. This gives me a good picture of where you're coming from. Now, let's do a quick assessment of your current English level through conversation."

⏱️ Timing: 4–6 minutes
Focus: Listen for patterns — does the student avoid certain tenses? Make consistent errors? Have strong vocabulary but weak grammar?

5. Level Assessment (Speaking)

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Assess student's current speaking ability
  • Identify grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation strengths and weaknesses
  • Determine if they are strong B1 or weak B1 (or possibly A2/B2)
⚠️ Important: This is not a formal test — it's a natural conversation designed to reveal the student's level. Don't tell them they're being assessed. Just have a conversation and listen carefully.

📝 Conversation Topics & Questions

Topic 1: Daily Life & Routines (Present Simple)

  • "Can you describe a typical day in your life? What do you usually do from morning to evening?"
  • "What do you enjoy doing on weekends? Do you have any regular activities or hobbies?"

Listen for: Present Simple accuracy, adverbs of frequency, vocabulary range

Topic 2: Past Experiences (Past Simple & Present Perfect)

  • "Tell me about something interesting or memorable that happened to you recently."
  • "Have you ever traveled to another country? If yes, where did you go? What was it like?"
  • "What's the best movie or TV show you've watched recently? What was it about?"

Listen for: Past Simple vs Present Perfect, irregular verbs, narrative ability

Topic 3: Future Plans & Hypotheticals (Future & Conditionals)

  • "What are your plans for the next few months? Do you have any trips, projects, or goals coming up?"
  • "If you had a free month with no obligations, what would you do? Where would you go?"
  • "What do you think you'll be doing in five years? Where do you see yourself?"

Listen for: Future forms (will, going to), Second Conditional, modal verbs

Topic 4: Opinions & Preferences (Complex Structures)

  • "What do you think is the most important skill to have in today's world? Why?"
  • "Do you prefer learning in a group or one-on-one? What are the advantages of each?"
  • "Some people say that learning English is essential. What's your opinion on that?"

Listen for: Ability to justify opinions, linking words, complex sentences

📊 What to Observe

Area What to Listen For Notes
Grammar Correct use of tenses, subject-verb agreement, word order
Vocabulary Range, precision, ability to paraphrase when stuck
Fluency Speed, pauses, self-correction, confidence
Pronunciation Clarity, intelligibility, problem sounds
Communication Ability to express ideas, ask for clarification, keep conversation going

Transition to Next Section:

"Thank you! I have a good sense of your English level now. Based on our conversation, I can see that you [mention 1–2 strengths]. We'll definitely work on [mention 1–2 areas for improvement] as we go forward."

"Now, let me explain how our course works, so you know what to expect."

⏱️ Timing: 5–8 minutes
Note-taking: Jot down specific errors, strengths, and approximate level (weak B1 / strong B1 / A2 / B2)

6. Explaining Course Format

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Clearly explain the course methodology
  • Set expectations about structure and flexibility
  • Highlight the unique features (Supernatural, scripts, songs, etc.)

Introduction to Format Explanation:

"Now, let me tell you about how our course works. I want you to understand our approach so you can decide if it's right for you."

Core Methodology:

"Our course is based on a communicative approach — that means we focus on using English in real-life situations, not just memorizing rules. We want you to actually speak and use English, not just know about it."

"We have a foundation textbook called American English File. This gives us structure and ensures we cover all the essential grammar, vocabulary, and skills systematically. I'll share the PDF with you before we start so you can see what it's like."

The Unique Part — Supernatural & Authentic Materials:

"Here's what makes our course different: while we use American English File for grammar and core vocabulary, most of our listening, reading, and discussion activities come from the TV series Supernatural and other authentic materials."

"We watch episodes, analyse scripts, discuss characters and plots, and even work with songs from the show. This makes learning more engaging and helps you hear real, natural English — not just textbook dialogues."

Flexibility & Pace:

"One important thing to understand: we don't rush through the material. We're not following a strict, fast-paced program where we have to finish a certain number of pages per week. Instead, we move at a pace that works for you."

"If you need more time on a grammar topic, we take it. If something is easy, we move through it quickly. The goal is mastery, not speed. We cover all the topics from the textbook, but we do it in a way that suits your learning style and speed."

Lesson Structure:

"Each lesson is 90 minutes — that's two academic hours. Typically, we have about 45 minutes of work, then a 5-minute break to rest, get water, stretch, and then another 45 minutes. However, this can be adjusted if needed."

"In a typical lesson, we might review homework, learn new vocabulary, study a grammar point, watch a clip from Supernatural, discuss it, and do some speaking or writing practice. Every lesson is different, but there's always a mix of skills."

Homework Options:

"We offer two formats: with homework or without homework. If you choose the homework format, you'll have assignments between lessons — usually reading, watching, vocabulary practice, or writing. This helps you progress faster because you're engaging with English regularly."

"If you choose the no-homework format, all practice happens during the lesson. This is fine, but progress may be slower because you only have 90 minutes of English per lesson. Many students find that a small amount of regular homework makes a big difference."

Check Understanding:

"Does that make sense? Do you have any questions about how the course works so far?"

⏱️ Timing: 4–6 minutes
Tone: Clear, confident, enthusiastic
Visual Aid (optional): Have the American English File PDF open to show if asked

7. Learning Preferences & Comfort

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Understand student's preferred learning style
  • Assess comfort level with different group compositions
  • Identify any specific needs or concerns

Learning Style & Preferences:

  • "How do you prefer to learn? Do you like more speaking activities, or do you prefer reading and writing?"
  • "Do you like working on grammar exercises, or do you find them boring? Would you prefer to learn grammar through conversation and examples?"
  • "Do you like receiving corrections while you're speaking, or would you prefer me to note errors and discuss them after you finish?"
  • "How do you feel about homework? Do you prefer having assignments between lessons, or would you rather do everything during class time?"
  • "Do you like working with visual materials (videos, images), or do you prefer text-based activities?"

Group Dynamics & Comfort:

  • "How do you feel about learning in a group with other students? Does it motivate you, or does it make you nervous?"
  • "If you're in a group, does it matter to you whether the group is mixed gender (both men and women), or would you prefer a group with only men or only women?"
  • "How do you feel about making mistakes in front of others? Does it bother you, or are you comfortable with it?"
  • "Do you prefer a competitive atmosphere (trying to be the best) or a collaborative one (working together to help each other)?"
  • "Would you feel comfortable speaking up in a group, or do you prefer one-on-one attention from the teacher?"

Additional Comfort & Needs:

  • "Are there any topics or activities you'd prefer to avoid? For example, certain types of discussions, personal questions, or specific content?"
  • "Do you have any learning differences or needs I should be aware of? For example, do you need more time for certain tasks, or do you prefer instructions in a particular format?"
  • "What time of day do you feel most alert and focused? Are mornings, afternoons, or evenings best for you?"
  • "Is there anything else that would make you feel more comfortable or help you learn better?"

Acknowledge & Reassure:

"Thank you for sharing all of that. It's really helpful to know your preferences. I'll make sure to take them into account in our lessons."

"It's completely normal to feel [nervous about groups / prefer one-on-one / etc.]. We can definitely accommodate that."

Transition to Next Section:

"Now, let's talk about your schedule and how often you'd like to have lessons."

⏱️ Timing: 4–6 minutes
Focus: Listen carefully to comfort level with groups — this is crucial for placement decision

8. Schedule & Frequency

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Determine how often the student wants to study
  • Identify available time slots
  • Set realistic expectations about progress

Schedule Questions:

  • "How often would you like to have English lessons? For example, once a week, twice a week, or more?"
  • "What days of the week work best for you? Are weekdays better, or weekends?"
  • "What time of day is most convenient for you? Morning, afternoon, or evening?"
  • "Do you have any regular commitments I should know about? For example, work shifts, other classes, family responsibilities?"
  • "Are you looking to start lessons immediately, or do you prefer to start at a specific date?"

Provide Guidance on Frequency:

"Based on your goals, I'd recommend [suggest frequency: e.g., 'two lessons per week' / 'one lesson per week']. Here's why: [explain briefly, e.g., 'With your goal of speaking fluently for work, more frequent lessons will help you progress faster' or 'Since you're busy with work, one lesson per week plus some homework is a good starting point']."

"Keep in mind that consistency is key. Regular lessons — even if they're just once a week — are more effective than irregular intensive study."

Set Expectations About Progress:

"Just so you know, language learning takes time. With regular lessons and some homework, most students see noticeable improvement in 2–3 months. Of course, it depends on your starting level, goals, and how much you practice outside of lessons."

"The important thing is to be patient with yourself and trust the process. We'll track your progress together, and you'll see improvement over time."

Transition to Next Section:

"Great, I have a good understanding of your schedule now. Let's talk a bit more about the homework options, since you mentioned [reference something they said earlier]."

⏱️ Timing: 3–5 minutes
Note-taking: Record preferred days, times, and frequency

10. Student Questions

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Give student opportunity to ask questions
  • Address any concerns or uncertainties
  • Build trust and transparency

Invite Questions:

"Now, I'd love to hear any questions you have. Is there anything you'd like to know about the course, the lessons, homework, scheduling, or anything else?"

"Don't hesitate to ask — no question is too small or silly. I want to make sure you feel comfortable and informed before we start."

Common Questions Students Ask (be prepared to answer):

  • "What happens if I miss a lesson?"
    (Have a clear policy ready: e.g., "Lessons can be rescheduled with 24 hours notice" or "Missed lessons are not refunded but can be made up if scheduled in advance.")
  • "Can I change my schedule later?"
    (e.g., "Yes, schedules can be adjusted as needed, but please let me know in advance so I can accommodate other students.")
  • "How do I pay for lessons?"
    (Explain your payment method: per lesson, monthly, upfront, etc.)
  • "What if I don't understand something in the lesson?"
    (e.g., "Just ask! I'm here to help. We can go over it again, find different explanations, or practice more.")
  • "Will I get feedback on my progress?"
    (e.g., "Yes, we'll have regular check-ins to discuss your progress, strengths, and areas to improve.")
  • "Can I switch from group to individual or vice versa?"
    (e.g., "Yes, that's possible. We can reassess after a few weeks and adjust if needed.")
  • "What materials do I need to buy?"
    (e.g., "I'll provide the American English File PDF. You don't need to buy anything else, but you might want a notebook and pen for taking notes.")

If Student Has No Questions:

"That's great — it sounds like everything is clear. If any questions come up later, feel free to ask at any time."

⏱️ Timing: 2–5 minutes (depending on number of questions)
Tone: Open, patient, transparent

11. Placement Decision

🎯 Goals of This Section

  • Make a clear recommendation (group or individual)
  • Explain the reasoning
  • Get student's agreement
  • Outline next steps

Summarize What You've Learned:

"Let me summarize what we've discussed today. Your main goal is [repeat their goal]. You have [mention their level: e.g., 'a solid B1 level with some areas to work on']. You prefer [mention their preferences: e.g., 'one-on-one attention / learning in a group']. Your schedule is [mention availability]."

Make Recommendation:

"Based on all of this, I recommend [group classes / individual lessons] for you. Here's why: [explain clearly, e.g., 'You're comfortable speaking in groups, and your level matches our B1 group well. You'll benefit from hearing other students and practicing together' or 'You prefer personalized attention, and your goals are quite specific, so individual lessons will allow us to focus exactly on what you need']."

Present Options:

"Here are your options:

  • Option 1: Join our B1 group class. The group meets on [days/times], and the next session starts on [date]. The group has [number] students, and they're all at a similar level to you."
  • Option 2: Start individual lessons. We can schedule lessons on [suggest days/times based on their availability], and we'll begin as soon as you're ready."
  • Option 3: If you're not sure, we can start with individual lessons for a few weeks and then reassess whether you'd like to join a group later."

"What do you think? Which option feels right for you?"

If Student Agrees:

"Great! I'm glad we found a good fit. Let me tell you about the next steps."

Outline Next Steps:

"Here's what happens next:

  • I'll send you a confirmation email with all the details: schedule, format, homework option, and payment information.
  • I'll also send you the American English File PDF so you can take a look before we start.
  • Our first lesson will be on [date/time]. I'll send you a reminder the day before.
  • If you have any questions before then, feel free to reach out."

If Student Needs Time to Decide:

"That's completely fine. Take your time to think about it. I'll send you a summary email with all the information we discussed, and you can let me know your decision within [suggest timeframe, e.g., 'a few days' / 'by the end of the week']."

"Feel free to reach out if you have any additional questions."

Closing the Lesson:

"Thank you so much for your time today. I really enjoyed getting to know you, and I'm excited about the possibility of working together. Have a great day, and I look forward to hearing from you soon!"

⏱️ Timing: 3–5 minutes
Tone: Confident, supportive, clear
Important: Don't pressure — let the student make the final decision

13. Appendices

📊 Level Indicators Quick Reference

Level Typical Characteristics
A2
  • Can communicate in simple, routine tasks
  • Limited vocabulary, frequent pauses
  • Basic sentence structures, many errors
  • Difficulty with past and future tenses
Weak B1
  • Can handle most everyday situations
  • Can describe experiences and give reasons
  • Inconsistent grammar accuracy
  • Limited vocabulary range, struggles with abstract topics
Strong B1
  • Can deal with most situations while traveling
  • Can produce simple connected text on familiar topics
  • Generally accurate grammar with some errors
  • Good vocabulary range, can paraphrase
B2
  • Can interact with fluency and spontaneity
  • Can produce clear, detailed text on complex subjects
  • Good grammatical control, occasional errors
  • Wide vocabulary, can discuss abstract topics

💬 Useful Phrases for Difficult Situations

If the student is very nervous:

  • "Please don't worry — this is just a conversation, not a test."
  • "Take your time. There's no rush."
  • "It's completely normal to feel nervous. Many students feel the same way."

If the student speaks very little:

  • "Can you tell me a bit more about that?"
  • "What do you think about...?"
  • "Have you ever experienced something like that?"

If the student asks about price:

  • "Let me explain our pricing structure..." [have your rates ready]
  • "We offer different packages depending on frequency and format. Let me send you the details after this lesson."

If the student wants to start immediately:

  • "Great! Let me check the available slots..."
  • "We can start as early as [date]. Does that work for you?"
⚠️ Final Reminder: This zero lesson is about building trust and understanding, not about impressing the student with your knowledge. Listen more than you speak, be genuine, and focus on their needs.

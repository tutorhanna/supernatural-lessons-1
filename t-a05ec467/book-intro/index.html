Lesson Overview

📌 Level B1 (Intermediate)
👤 Age Group Adults (18+)
⏱️ Lesson Length 60–90–120 minutes (adjustable)
🎯 Main Skills Speaking, listening, grammar, vocabulary
📄 Materials Needed Student worksheet (shared docs), this Teacher's Guide, Invitation lesson, extra audio/video
📖 Lesson Type Introductory / Diagnostic / Ice-breaker

🎯 Lesson Aims:

  • To introduce the course concept: English through Supernatural and American English File
  • To create a comfortable, engaging atmosphere for adult learners
  • To assess students' current English level (grammar, vocabulary, speaking)
  • To explore students' connection to the show and their personal goals
  • To introduce key vocabulary and grammar structures

📖 Course Structure

  • We follow American English File and the Supernatural TV show.
  • We divide episodes into several parts and watch them gradually throughout the unit.
  • We watch in original English, but sometimes there will be homework tasks to watch in Russian dubbing and compare the original with the translation.
  • We will practise doing an English voiceover for a Russian clip or dubbing the English episode into Russian to understand the differences in style and meaning.

🎯 Learning Outcomes

  • ✅ Students can introduce themselves and ask basic personal questions.
  • ✅ Students can talk about their TV viewing habits and preferences.
  • ✅ Students can identify their strengths and weaknesses in English grammar.
  • ✅ Students understand the course format and expectations.

Lesson Introduction

🎯 Aim: To welcome students, set expectations, and create a comfortable atmosphere.

🎬 Teacher's Script — What to Say (5-7 minutes)

📌 STEP 1: Greeting and Setting the Mood

"Good morning/afternoon/evening, everyone! Welcome, hunters! Welcome to Supernatural English. My name is [your name], and I'm so happy to see you here today."

"This is a special course where we combine learning English with the iconic TV show Supernatural and the textbook American English File. We'll watch clips, read scripts, discuss characters, and learn vocabulary and grammar — all while exploring the Winchester brothers' world."

"But first, I'd like to get to know you a little bit."

📌 STEP 2: How Are You Feeling Today?

"Before we dive into the lesson, let's do a quick check-in. How are you feeling today? Are you tired? Excited? Nervous? Please share a few words. This is a safe space, so feel free to share."

(Wait for students to respond. If they are shy, share first: "I'm feeling great today because I love teaching this course!")

💬 Useful Language: I'm feeling great / fine / good / well / a bit tired / excited / nervous.

Suggested opening script:

💬 Useful Language for Students:

  • I'm feeling great / fine / good / well.
  • I'm a bit tired / nervous / excited.
  • I'm looking forward to this lesson!
  • I've had a long day, but I'm ready to study.
  • I'm a little stressed, but I'm happy to be here.

"We'll be using Supernatural as the theme for this course. Have you heard of the show? Have you watched it? If not, don't worry — you'll still enjoy the lessons!"

📚 Grammar Explanations with Additional Examples

1. Present Simple

Use: For habits, routines, facts, and general truths.

Form: I/You/We/They work | He/She/It works

Additional Examples:

  • I drink coffee every morning. (habit)
  • Water boils at 100°C. (fact)
  • She works in a hospital. (routine)
  • They don't eat meat. (negative)
  • Does he speak French? (question)

Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays, rarely

2. Present Continuous

Use: For actions happening now, temporary situations, and changes.

Form: am/is/are + verb-ing

Additional Examples:

  • I'm reading a book right now. (action now)
  • I'm staying with my sister this week. (temporary)
  • The weather is getting colder. (change)
  • She's cooking dinner at the moment. (now)
  • They are studying English this semester. (temporary)

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, currently

3. Past Simple

Use: For completed actions in the past.

Form: Regular verbs: verb + -ed | Irregular verbs: see the list

Additional Examples:

  • I visited London last year. (regular)
  • She went to the cinema yesterday. (irregular)
  • We didn't like the movie. (negative)
  • Did you see the news? (question)
  • He studied English for two years. (duration)

Signal words: yesterday, last week, in 2010, two days ago, when I was a child

4. Present Perfect

Use: For past actions with a connection to the present.

Form: have/has + past participle

Additional Examples:

  • I have been to Paris. (experience)
  • She has already eaten. (finished recently)
  • They haven't finished yet. (not completed)
  • Have you ever tried sushi? (question)
  • He has lived here for five years. (duration)

Signal words: ever, never, already, yet, just, since, for, recently

5. Conditionals

First Conditional (real possibility): If + Present Simple, will + infinitive

Examples:

  • If it rains, I will stay home.
  • If you study, you will pass the exam.
  • She will be happy if he comes.

Second Conditional (unreal situation): If + Past Simple, would + infinitive

Examples:

  • If I were rich, I would travel the world.
  • If I had more time, I would learn another language.
  • What would you do if you won the lottery?

Important: In Second Conditional, were is used instead of was for all subjects in formal English.

6. Passive Voice

Use: To focus on the action or the object, not the subject.

Form: to be + past participle

Additional Examples:

  • The letter was written by Maria. (Past Simple passive)
  • The house is being renovated. (Present Continuous passive)
  • The project will be finished next week. (Future passive)
  • English is spoken all over the world. (Present Simple passive)
  • The new restaurant was opened last month. (Past Simple passive)

Use: When the subject is not important or obvious, or when we want to be formal.

7. Used To

Use: To talk about past habits or states that are no longer true.

Form: used to + infinitive

Additional Examples:

  • I used to play tennis, but I don't now.
  • She used to live in the countryside.
  • Did you use to have a pet? (question)
  • I didn't use to like coffee. (negative)
  • He used to be a teacher, but now he's a writer.

Note: Used to only exists in the past. To talk about present habits, use usually or the Present Simple.

8. Modal Verbs (might)

Use: To express possibility, permission, ability, advice, etc.

Examples:

  • I might go to the cinema tonight. (possibility — maybe)
  • She might be late. (possibility)
  • We might have a party next weekend. (possibility)
  • He might not come to the meeting. (negative)
  • What might happen if we fail? (question)

Other modal verbs: can, could, may, must, should, will, would

9. Reported Speech

Use: To report what someone said, without quoting them directly.

Examples:

  • Direct: "I am a teacher." → Reported: She said she was a teacher.
  • Direct: "I will help you." → Reported: He said he would help me.
  • Direct: "I have seen the movie." → Reported: She said she had seen the movie.
  • Direct: "I don't like coffee." → Reported: He said he didn't like coffee.
  • Direct: "We are studying." → Reported: They said they were studying.

Key changes: Present Simple → Past Simple, will → would, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, can → could

    • Now look at the word "when" (когда). Can we use the future word "will" right after "when" or "if" in time clauses?👉 Short answer: No, we cannot use "will" after "when" or "if".
    • Since we cannot use "will", what tense (время) must we use instead?👉 Short answer: We must use the Present Simple tense.
    • So, why do we say "when you ARE done" instead of "when you will be done"?👉 Short answer: Because grammar rules forbid "will" after "when", so we use the present verb "are".

    Additional Examples:

    • There'll be a lot of food at the party.
    • There'll be traffic at this hour.
    • There'll be no secrets between us.

    Common mistake: Using "There will be" in a time clause (e.g., "when there will be" — incorrect). Use Present Simple in time clauses: "when there is".

    Past Continuous vs Past Simple: "I was soaring ever higher, but I flew too high."

    Grammar Explanation for the Teacher: Here we have a contrast between two past tenses. "I was soaring" is Past Continuous. It focuses on the continuous process (длительный процесс в развитии) and the feeling of flying. The word "ever" before a comparative (higher) means "continuously" or "more and more" (всё выше и выше). "I flew" is Past Simple. It shows a sudden completed action or result (конкретный факт/результат в прошлом) that interrupted or ended the process.

    Let's look at WAS SOARING and FLEW

    💬 Follow-up questions to help students understand the grammar:

    • What is the base verb (начальная форма) for "soaring" and "flew"?
      👉 Short answer: The base verbs are "soar" (парить/лететь) and "fly" (летать).
    • Which part of the sentence describes a long, continuous process (длительный процесс во времени)?
      👉 Short answer: "I was soaring" (Past Continuous).
    • What does the word "ever" mean when we say "ever higher"? Does it mean "always" or "more and more"?
      👉 Short answer: It means "more and more" (всё выше и выше / непрерывно).
    • Which part of the sentence shows a single completed fact or a sudden result (конкретное законченное действие)?
      👉 Short answer: "I flew too high" (Past Simple).
    • What is the difference between "high" and "too high"? Does "too" mean a good thing or a negative problem?
      👉 Short answer: "Too" means more than enough/excessive (слишком — это несёт негативный оттенок/проблему).
    • Metaphorically, does "soaring higher" mean the singer was becoming more successful or more depressed?
      👉 Short answer: It means he was becoming very successful and proud (взлетал к успеху), but then he made a mistake because of his ego.

    Real-Life Examples:

    1. Using "Past Continuous + but + Past Simple" for processes interrupted by a mistake or event:

    • I was driving faster and faster, but I missed the turn. (Я ехал всё быстрее, но пропустил поворот).
    • She was spending more and more money, but she spent too much. (Она тратила всё больше денег, но потратила слишком много).

      • Additional Examples:

      • I was cooking dinner, but I burnt the meat.
      • I was driving to work, but I got a flat tyre.
      • I was writing an email, but my laptop died.

      2. Using "ever + comparative" (всё более / всё сильнее):

      • The weather is getting ever colder. (Погода становится всё холоднее).
      • Prices are growing ever higher. (Цены растут всё выше и выше).

    Grammar: Past Continuous (was soaring) describes a long background action. Past Simple (flew) describes a short, completed action that interrupts it.

    9. Song Meaning & Real-Life Application

    🎯 The Anthem of Resilience

    The legendary 1976 rock anthem "Carry On Wayward Son" by Kansas is more than just a classic song. It is an incredible tool for mastering English. Its powerful metaphors perfectly describe the tragic, monster-hunting lives of Sam and Dean Winchester from Supernatural, and the lyrics also work as practical, real-world conversational English.

    📖 This guide explains every single line of the song — analysing its hidden depth, its meaning, grammar, and how you can apply these structures to your own daily life.

    🎯 Act I: The Anthem of Resilience (The Chorus)

    Carry on, my wayward son
    There'll be peace when you are done
    Lay your weary head to rest
    Don't you cry no more

    1. "Carry on, my wayward son"

    The Deep Meaning: To "carry on" means to keep going despite difficulties, tragedy, or fate. A "wayward" person is someone who is difficult to control, who goes their own way: unpredictable, rebellious. In Supernatural, this perfectly describes the Winchester brothers — constantly fighting, breaking the rules of Heaven and Hell, and refusing to give up.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "Sorry for interrupting, please carry on."
    • "Even when things are hard, you must carry on."
    • "He was a wayward teenager, but now he's a doctor."
    • "My cat is very wayward — she never listens."

    2. "There'll be peace when you are done"

    The Deep Meaning: For an outsider, this looks like a promise of a happy ending. For a hunter, it is a tragic omen (a sad truth). The only way the battle truly ends is with death or the end of the road.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "There'll be no problems as long as you follow the instructions."
    • "There'll be a celebration when we finish this project."
    • "I will call you when I arrive."
    • "Please wait here until I come back."

    3. "Lay your weary head to rest"

    The Deep Meaning: A call to find comfort after an overwhelming struggle. "Weary" means very tired — not just physically, but mentally and emotionally exhausted. This line is an invitation to find rest after a long struggle.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "After a 12-hour shift, I was completely weary."
    • "I am weary of all this drama at work."
    • "She looked weary after weeks of studying for exams."

    4. "Don't you cry no more"

    The Deep Meaning: A comforting command to stop grieving and stand strong.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "Don't you worry about what they think."
    • "Don't you give up on your dreams!"
    • "Don't you worry — everything will be fine."
    • "Don't you forget about me!"

    🎯 Act II: The Conflict of the Mind (Verse 1)

    Once I rose above the noise and confusion
    Just to get a glimpse of beyond this illusion
    I was soaring ever higher, but I flew too high
    Though my eyes could see, I still was a blind man
    Though my mind could think, I still was a mad man
    I hear the voices when I'm dreaming
    I can hear them say...

    5. "Once I rose above the noise and confusion"

    The Deep Meaning: Stepping back from the chaotic details of daily life to see the "big picture" or find clarity.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "You need to rise above the office gossip and focus on your work."
    • "I chose to rise above his rude comments and stayed polite."
    • "She rose above all the criticism and became successful."
    • "It's important to rise above petty arguments."

    6. "Just to get a glimpse of beyond this illusion"

    The Deep Meaning: Realizing that the world around you might not be what it seems—a core theme in a show where monsters hide in plain sight.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "I only caught a glimpse of the new UI design, but it looks promising."
    • "The results went far beyond our expectations."
    • "She got a glimpse of her future and it inspired her."

    7. "I was soaring ever higher, but I flew too high"

    The Deep Meaning: A classic reference to the myth of Icarus — he flew too close to the sun and fell. It means being overconfident, taking too many risks, and then failing.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "I was doing great at the interview, but I made a terrible mistake at the very end."
    • "We were expanding our business rapidly, but we ran out of budget."
    • "She was driving too fast and crashed."

    8. "Though my eyes could see, I still was a blind man / Though my mind could think, I still was a mad man"

    The Deep Meaning: This highlights an intense internal crisis. You can have perfect vision and high intelligence, but still be completely blind to your own destiny, or act completely irrational out of love, duty, or obsession.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "Though he had a GPS, he still got lost."
    • "Though she practised every day, she felt nervous."
    • "Though it was raining, we went for a walk."

    🎯 Act III: The Trap of Pretending (Verse 2)

    Masqueraded as a man with a reason
    My charade was the event of the season
    And if I claim to be a wise man, well, it surely means that I don't know
    On a stormy sea of moving emotion
    Tossed about, I'm like a ship on the ocean
    I set a course for winds of fortune
    But I hear the voices say...

    9. "Masqueraded as a man with a reason"

    The Deep Meaning: Faking confidence. Pretending you have everything under control and know exactly what you are doing, even when you feel completely lost inside.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "Be careful online; many scammers masquerade as bank agents."
    • "Sometimes I feel like I'm just masquerading as a real adult."
    • "He masqueraded as an expert, but he knew nothing."

    10. "My charade was the event of the season"

    The Deep Meaning: A "charade" is a fake show, an act. The singer is joking that his act of pretending to be okay was so obvious that everyone noticed.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "Let's stop this charade and say what we actually think."
    • "The whole interview process felt like a charade because they already chose an internal candidate."
    • "The meeting was a charade — they already made a decision."
    • "I'm tired of this charade. Let's be honest with each other."

    11. "And if I claim to be a wise man, well, it surely means that I don't know"

    The Deep Meaning: The ultimate rock-and-roll nod to the Socratic paradox. True wisdom begins with admitting your own limitations. The moment you brag about knowing everything, you prove that you actually know nothing.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "He claims to be a senior developer, but he struggled with basic syntax."
    • "If someone claims to be an absolute expert in AI, they probably don't know its limitations."
    • "If someone claims to be perfect, they're lying."
    • "She claims to be a professional, but she made many mistakes."

    12. "On a stormy sea of moving emotion / Tossed about, I'm like a ship on the ocean"

    The Deep Meaning: This striking visual captures the feeling of being overwhelmed by circumstances beyond your control. It represents the chaotic lifestyle of a hunter, constantly dragged from one crisis to another.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "My travel plans were completely tossed about by the strike."
    • "Employees feel tossed about by all the changes."
    • "I feel tossed about by all these new responsibilities."

    13. "I set a course for winds of fortune"

    The Deep Meaning: Making a decision to change your life, take a risk, and go after success and a better future.

    💬 Real-Life Application:

    • "She set a course for a career in technology."
    • "The company set a course for international growth."
    • "After graduation, he set a course for a new life abroad."

    • "Nothing equals the feeling of finishing a big project."
